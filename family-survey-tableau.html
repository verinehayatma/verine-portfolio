Project Overview

From raw survey exports to a stakeholder-ready story

The Edmonds School District family survey generated a large, messy dataset — the kind that's easy to misread if it isn't cleaned and structured carefully before it reaches a dashboard.

What I did

I designed Tableau dashboards from over 7,000 family survey responses to visualize key performance indicators for the district. Before any visualization work, I cleaned and validated the dataset to improve reporting accuracy and analytical reliability — catching inconsistencies that would have skewed the KPIs if left unaddressed.

The final deliverable was a Tableau story: a guided sequence of dashboards presenting the findings in a format built for stakeholder decision-making rather than raw data exploration. Among the KPIs it surfaced, family awareness of bullying policies and reporting channels stood out as a metric the district could track and act on directly — turning a single survey question into an ongoing measure of how well school-to-home communication is working.

  • Data source7,000+ family survey responses
  • ToolsTableau, data cleaning & validation
  • OutputKPI dashboards + Tableau story

Why it mattered

Bullying awareness was one of the key indicators buried in the survey data. Surfacing it clearly gave the district a way to see how aware families actually were of bullying policies and reporting channels — not just anecdotally, but as a trackable number. That visibility supports two things at once: it gives the district a baseline to measure whether future outreach efforts are actually improving awareness, and it strengthens the broader communication loop between school and home, since the same dashboard reflects how well families feel informed overall. In that sense, the KPI isn't just a metric — it's a feedback signal the district can act on.

48% vs. 15%

Agreement on bullying-policy awareness ranged from 48% at the highest-scoring school to just 15% at the lowest — turning a vague sense of "some schools communicate better" into a specific, trackable gap.

Key Finding

Awareness of bullying policy varies more than the district might expect

Across the roughly 30 schools surveyed, agreement that students and families are aware of bullying policies and reporting channels was inconsistent — and at most schools, "disagree" was the largest single response.

Bullying-policy awareness: best vs. worst vs. district average

Scriber Lake High led the district; Edmonds Heights K-12 trailed it by more than 30 percentage points.
