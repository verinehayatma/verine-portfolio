Project Overview

Helping a local BBQ spot see its customers clearly

Carolina Smoke BBQ wanted to grow its catering business and build stronger customer loyalty, but didn't know which levers mattered most. Our team designed and ran an original study to find out.

The brief

Carolina Smoke differentiates itself with a Carolina-style rub instead of the more common Texas sauce-forward approach, and has leaned into catering as dine-in traffic softened post-pandemic. Management needed to know what actually moves the needle on customer satisfaction and repeat visits before investing further in marketing or training.

We translated that decision problem into five research problems covering awareness, choice drivers, satisfaction, marketing channels, and pricing — then built a Qualtrics survey with skip logic so we only asked satisfaction questions of people who'd actually eaten there.

Who we heard from

87 responses, concentrated in Carolina Smoke's backyard

Recruitment ran through the r/Bothell subreddit. The sample skewed male (63.9%) and middle-aged, with over half of respondents living in the 98011, 98012, and 98021 ZIP codes — Bothell, Kenmore, and Mill Creek.

Respondent age distribution

35–44 year-olds were the largest single group, consistent with Carolina Smoke's existing customer base.

Key Findings

Food quality and service outrank everything else

Two questions cut through the noise: what do people say matters, and how do they actually find the restaurant?

How important is each factor when choosing a restaurant?

Food quality dominates the "very important" category — no other factor comes close.

Which channels do people use to find restaurants?

Google Maps and word of mouth are the two clear leaders — far ahead of review sites and social platforms.

4.70 / 5

Average importance rating for food quality when choosing a restaurant — the single highest-scoring factor in the entire survey, with low variance (SD = 0.73), meaning respondents agreed almost unanimously.

Statistical Analysis

What the numbers confirmed

We ran a t-test on word-of-mouth referrals and a multiple regression on the five perception drivers of satisfaction.

Independent samples t-test

Word of mouth drives visit intent

Customers who first heard about Carolina Smoke from friends or family reported meaningfully higher intent to visit again in the next month (M = 3.25) than those who heard about it another way (M = 2.46).

p = 0.026 < 0.05 — significant
Multiple regression · R² = 0.869

Three factors drive satisfaction

Staff service, food quality, and price fairness all significantly predict overall satisfaction. Comfort and order accuracy did not.

  • Staff service+0.534, p < .001
  • Food quality+0.314, p < .001
  • Price value+0.258, p = .012
  • Order prep+0.145, p = .263 (n.s.)
  • Comfort−0.107, p = .535 (n.s.)
Recommendations

What we told Carolina Smoke to do next

The data pointed away from big ad spend and toward doubling down on fundamentals.

Train for consistency

Build a staff service program focused on attentiveness and communication during busy periods — the single strongest predictor of satisfaction.

Protect food quality

Introduce periodic quality checks and portion guidelines so every plate matches expectations, visit after visit.

Reframe price, don't cut it

Value combos and highlighting ingredient quality address perceived fairness without discounting the brand.

Fuel word of mouth

Local partnerships with butchers and farms give loyal customers a reason to talk about Carolina Smoke.

Keep Google Maps current

It's the #1 discovery channel — accurate hours, photos, and menu links matter more than paid ads.

Deprioritize review-site spend

Yelp and similar platforms had far less reach than Maps or referrals — a lower-ROI place to invest limited marketing dollars.
